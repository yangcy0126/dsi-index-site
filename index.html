Method

How Diplomatic Text Becomes DSI

The site weights documents equally for WDSI in all 15 economies. The paper's international WDSI averages 14 daily scores, taking the most adverse score on each publication day and carrying it forward on silent days. Both are labeled in Data Downloads. EDSI and ODSI retain their original definitions.

01

Collect and clean official texts

The panel draws on official foreign-ministry and foreign-office communications: press briefings, statements, written responses, and other official releases. The source formats and periods of coverage vary by country.

02

Score war-related rhetoric

Repeated judgments identify war relevance, direction, and intensity, with reconciliation and human validation. WDSI ranges from -3 (most conciliatory) to +3 (most adverse). Non-war content receives zero.

03

Give each document equal weight

Sum the scores of all accepted documents from the date shown and the preceding 13 calendar days, then divide by the number of documents. A day with three releases contributes three scores. A zero score counts; a day without a release adds no observation.

04

Report daily and period values

The first 13 dates are blank. If a full window contains no documents, the last available index carries forward. The raw series averages only that day's documents. Monthly and quarterly values average the available daily indices, not all documents pooled across the month or quarter. Partial periods are flagged.

Why It Matters

What the Paper Finds

The November 19 paper weights documents equally for Stock Connect and days equally in its international tables. The international weighting comparison applies document weights to the same historical samples, with the same outcomes, controls and inference. It is a separate check, not a revision to the paper.

Stock Connect transactions

Table 4 already uses document weights. At sample means, a one-standard-deviation deterioration is associated with about USD 24 million less in northbound net purchases and USD 35 million less in net flow per trading day. The daily southbound estimate is not significant. Within press conferences, more adverse answers are followed by faster southbound accumulation (Table 5).

Equity transactions

Under either weighting, more adverse rhetoric accompanies lower quarterly net equity inflows in Australia and larger quarterly purchases of foreign equities by Brazilian residents. Spain's monthly net inflow estimate remains negative, but is no longer significant in the main specification: p = 0.009 in Table 8 and 0.111 with document weights.

Debt transactions

Germany's net debt inflows rise at both frequencies under either weighting. The declines in South Korea and Brazil reported in Table 9 remain significant quarterly, but not monthly, with document weights. Among the main equity and debt net-flow tests, only German quarterly debt passes both cross-country corrections at 5% (BH and Holm; adjusted p = 0.034).

U.S. cross-border equity holdings

Adverse global rhetoric is more strongly associated with higher U.S. net equity holdings than U.S.-specific rhetoric. Using the equal-weight factor from the other 14 economies, the difference between the two coefficients is 25.68 in Table 10 and 19.06 with document weights (p = 0.003). These are holdings at market value, not new purchases.
